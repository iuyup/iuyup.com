## A Short English Guide to the Original

The original essay, **“如何超过大多数人” (“How to Get Ahead of Most People”)**, was written by **Chen Hao** and published on **CoolShell** in 2019. It uses satire and reverse psychology to expose habits that quietly hold people back: consuming low-quality information, mistaking fragmented reading for learning, chasing shortcuts, repeating work without reflection, and letting comparison or comfort replace long-term growth.

Its constructive argument is that progress comes from four connected areas: cognition, knowledge, skill, and leadership. Better information sources improve what you can see; systematic learning turns scattered facts into knowledge; deliberate practice, mistakes, and feedback turn knowledge into skill; and high standards, genuine interests, strong habits, and persistence make those abilities compound over time.

This page is only a brief English overview, not a translation or reproduction of the essay. For the full argument, examples, and the author's original wording, please read [Chen Hao's article on CoolShell](https://coolshell.cn/articles/19464.html).
